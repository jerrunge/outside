The clock is the real decision

Not cost, not scenery. These three have completely different booking physics, and two of them cannot be locked on the same timeline. Read this before anything else on the page.

Kumano Kodo
Decide this week
Roughly 3 months out, in peak season

The tourism bureau tells people to book about six months ahead for peak seasons, and autumn counts as peak. You are already inside that. Worse, the Kohechi will not confirm a partial itinerary: every village night has to be secured or the request is refused outright. If this is the one, the request goes in now and the answer comes back in days to weeks, not instantly.

Southern Appalachian Trail
Cannot book yet
Permits open 30 days out

Great Smoky Mountains backcountry permits release exactly 30 days before entry, now through Recreation.gov rather than the old park system. You physically cannot lock November today. That sounds like a downside and is actually the opposite: this option costs you nothing to hold while the move sorts itself out, and November is deep off season.

Te Araroa
Nothing to book
Gated by conditions, not inventory

Huts are first come, there is no permit lottery, and the only paperwork is an NZeTA plus the visitor levy with about 72 hours of processing. Which means the decision is never forced by a booking window. It is forced by whether the snow is off the passes and the rivers are down, and that answer is not knowable until you are close.

What changed under verification

Four findings that the working comparison either missed or got backwards. Two of them change the ranking.

Correction 1 · The Kumano window inverts, it does not improve

Original assumption: pushing later into November is neutral or mildly positive for Japan.

It is negative. Autumn colour on the Kii Peninsula arrives later than most of Japan, and mid to late November is the actual peak. Operators now report as many trekkers in that stretch as in cherry blossom season. So Window B makes Kumano harder to book, not easier, at exactly the moment the Kohechi bottleneck villages are most likely to be gone. If Kumano is the pick, Window A is the better bet, which is the reverse of the pattern for the other two.

Correction 2 · The Miura-guchi workaround does not apply to your route

Original assumption: if Miura-guchi is unavailable there is an official transport workaround using Totsukawa as a base.

The workaround is real. It is also northbound only. The Kohechi bus out of Totsukawa exists specifically for walkers going Hongu toward Koyasan, and your itinerary runs the other way, Koyasan down to Hongu. So if Miura-guchi is full in your window, the fix is not a shuttle, it is flipping the entire route to walk south to north. That is a legitimate option and it works, but it inverts the arc: you would finish at Koyasan among the temples instead of at Nachi waterfall and the ocean. Worth knowing that is the trade before you find out at booking time.

Correction 3 · You do not qualify for the AT thru-hiker permit

Original plan: Winding Stair Gap to Davenport Gap, roughly 130 miles, thru-hiker permit assumed.

The Smokies thru-hiker permit requires you to begin and end at least 50 miles outside the park. Winding Stair Gap qualifies on the front end. Davenport Gap does not, because it sits on the park boundary. As written, this is a General Backcountry Permit trip, and General permit holders may not tent at shelters, they need a reserved space in one of the twelve AT shelters every single night inside the park, one night per shelter, no repeats. The four spaces held for thru-hikers only apply from mid March to mid June, so they are not there for you either. Two clean fixes exist and both are laid out below.

Correction 4 · New Zealand in November is emptier, not fuller. I had this wrong too.

My own earlier note to you: that mid November would bring more parties through the Richmond Ranges and better hut company.

That is not how the trail's timing works. Southbound thru-hikers leave Cape Reinga between late October and early December and do not reach the South Island until December or January. Northbounders start in January or February. In November the Richmond Ranges are close to empty. The conditions genuinely do improve as you move later, but the social upside I described does not exist, and the hut-company argument that made Te Araroa look like a viable reset trip goes with it. Full detail in the third block, including why this matters more for you specifically than it would for most people.

Why this one, for this particular trip

The rhythm is the whole argument. Walk alone for six to nine hours through cedar and stone and empty mountain, arrive somewhere small where a person is expecting you, eat food you did not have to plan, sit in hot water, sleep under a heavy futon, then disappear back into the mountains at dawn. You get the solitude without handing yourself fourteen consecutive evenings alone in the dark with a headlamp and your own thoughts, which given what you are walking away from is not a small design consideration.

It is also the only one of the three that does not ask your body for a second full-pack expedition three weeks after the Lost Coast. The climbing is serious and the days are long, but the load is a day pack, and the Omata to Miura-guchi leg has an official luggage shuttle if you book it at least 20 days ahead.

The gate: Kumano Travel will not confirm a partial Kohechi. Reservations are required in Koyasan, Omata, Miura-guchi and Totsukawa Onsen, and if they cannot confirm the whole chain they decline the request rather than leave you exposed on a route with four thousand-metre passes and no bail points. Miura-guchi has two properties, total. Koguchi is the same story one stage later. Book those two first and build the rest of the trip, and the flights, around whatever comes back.
If Miura-guchi is gone: flip the route and walk northbound, Hongu up to Koyasan. That unlocks the Kohechi bus out of Totsukawa, which is northbound only, and lets you sleep two nights in Totsukawa instead. You lose the ocean finish at Nachi and end among the temples instead. Different trip, still a very good one.

Conditions and cost

  • Daytime temperatures around 10 to 15 C. Cold mornings on the high passes, wet stone after rain, early dark.
  • The Kohechi closes roughly mid December to mid March for snow. November is inside the season with room to spare.
  • Ground cost $1,800 to $2,700, most of it lodging, with many dinners and breakfasts included in the room rate.
  • Reservations close 20 days before check-in. Cancellation terms vary by property and some are request-only rather than instant.

What being based in Indiana does to this option

It removes the airport. Indianapolis to Winding Stair Gap is roughly eight hours, which means you drive your own car to your own trailhead, on your own schedule, with a shuttle only needed at the far end. If the move gets messy, if Maddy needs you, if your parents need you, you are one drive from being back, not one international flight and three connections. Nothing else on this page gives you that.

It also collapses the cost to a rounding error, though you have told me not to optimise for that. The more relevant effect is that this trip has no commitment cost until 30 days before you walk, which is the only one of the three you can genuinely hold in reserve while the rest of your life resolves.

Fixing the permit problem

As originally drawn, Winding Stair Gap to Davenport Gap does not qualify for the thru-hiker permit, and a General Backcountry Permit forces a reserved shelter space every night in the park with no tenting fallback. Two ways through:

  • Fix A, recommended. Extend to Hot Springs, then on toward Erwin. Continuing past Davenport Gap puts your finish more than 50 miles outside the park boundary, which qualifies you for the $44.50 thru-hiker permit. That permit gives you an eight day window through the Smokies, a 30 day flex range around your estimated entry date, and the right to tent beside a shelter if it is full. It also adds Hot Springs, an actual trail town with a bed and a hot meal and other humans, right when you would most want one. This is the version that solves the permit and the evenings at the same time.
  • Fix B. Keep it at 130 miles and book shelters. General permit, $8 per person per night plus a $6 transaction fee, reserving each of the twelve AT shelters you need by name and date. Note the hard cap: a single General permit covers a maximum of 7 nights. In November availability is very likely fine. It is simply less forgiving, since a bad weather day that puts you behind schedule puts you off your reserved shelter with nowhere legal to pitch, and shelter reservations mean sleeping inside, with tents prohibited both outside and inside.
The real risk on this one is not the terrain. It is the evenings. Dark by 5:30, nights in the 20s at elevation, and in November you may well have the shelter to yourself for most of two weeks. For most trips that is the reward. For this trip, three weeks out from a separation and a move, fourteen straight nights alone in the cold with nothing to do but think is the exact failure mode to design against. The Hot Springs extension, and deliberately taking a room at the Nantahala Outdoor Center and at Fontana, are not luxuries here. They are the structure.

What it is

The scenery winner, and it is not close. Sharp ridges, alpine basins, beech forest and tussock, glacial valleys, remote huts, the whole South Island vocabulary. The Richmond Ranges into St Arnaud and on through Nelson Lakes is the closest thing on earth to what you are imagining when you say you want to walk until something in you resets.

Why November does not work, stated plainly

The Department of Conservation describes this specifically as one of the hardest parts of Te Araroa: extreme alpine weather, many unbridged river crossings, steep drop-offs, and the explicit warning that snow, freezing temperatures and flooded rivers occur even in summer. The guidance is to carry supplies for at least ten days precisely because you may need to sit still for several of them waiting for a river to drop. The entire trail's seasonal design exists to put walkers over the Richmond passes and Waiau Pass snow-free, and that means December at the earliest.

And the correction I owe you: the huts will not be busy. Southbound thru-hikers start at Cape Reinga between late October and early December and reach this country in December or January. Northbounders begin in January or February. Through November you would be moving through the most technically demanding stretch of the trail, alone, on an eight to nine day food carry, in an early season snowpack, with the huts largely empty and no realistic bail-out short of walking yourself out. That is not a solitude problem. That is a margin problem.

The disqualifier is not the trail. It is who is waiting. Every other risk here is one you have the skills to manage and have managed before. But this is the one option where a bad river crossing or a rolled ankle means days before anyone knows, on a different continent, while your daughter is at your parents' house in the middle of a move. That calculus is different than it would have been two years ago, and it is different than it will be next year.

If you take it anyway, or take it later

  • Carry a tent regardless. The correct framing is tent equals guaranteed bed, hut equals upgrade.
  • NZeTA is NZ$17 through the mobile app or NZ$23 online, plus the NZ$100 International Visitor Levy, paid in the same transaction. Allow 72 hours for processing. Neither figure appeared in the original cost table.
  • Ground cost $1,400 to $2,200 excluding flights, including the levy, hut and trail passes, shuttles and town nights.
  • The real recommendation: put this on the calendar for late January or February 2027 instead. Passes clear, rivers drop, huts fill with people walking the same direction as you, and you will be a year further into whatever the move turns out to be. It is the right trip. It is not the right November.

How to actually book each one

Three completely different systems. One is a request queue run by humans, one is a federal reservation portal, one is barely a system at all. Follow the block for whichever you pick.

Booking mechanics
Kumano Kodo · KUMANO TRAVEL
Request-based · Not instant · Act this week

This is not a booking site. You are asking Tanabe City tourism staff to telephone small family-run inns on your behalf, because most of these places do not use computers. Nothing is held or stocked. Understand that and the process makes sense.

The sequence

  1. Create a MyPage account at kumano-travel.com. Sign In, then "Create your account" near the bottom. Confirm through the email link, and a second email gives you your Sign In ID. Do this tonight, on its own, so it is not in the way later. Whitelist their address at the same time. The whole process runs on four separate emails and one landing in spam kills the reservation.
  2. Build the cart, with alternates. Search each night and add plans like a normal shop. For Miura-guchi and Koguchi, add every property listed, not just your favourite. Staff will substitute if your first choice is full and you want them working from your list rather than guessing. Put the Omata to Miura-guchi luggage shuttle in the same cart.
  3. Submit between 9am and 5pm Japan time. You can browse any hour, but requests only send during Japanese business hours. From Pacifica that is 5pm to 1am the evening before. From Indiana it is 8pm to 4am. You get an immediate acknowledgment email. That is not a booking.
  4. Wait for the "Processed Reservation Request" email. Usually under three working days, sometimes a week. This is where you learn what they actually secured and what got substituted, and it is the moment you find out whether the Kohechi chain has a hole in it.
  5. Pay within 48 hours. Sign into MyPage, hit "Pay to Confirm" beside the reservation number, review contents and price, scroll to the bottom of the terms until the agree box appears, pay by card. Miss the window and the entire reservation cancels automatically and you start over. Put the deadline in your calendar the minute that email lands.
  6. Download and print every voucher. MyPage gives you Download Voucher and Download Reservation Outline after payment. The voucher is what you hand the innkeeper at check-in and the luggage service at drop-off, and the NOTES section on the back carries your pickup times and locations. Print them. Do not rely on a phone in a valley with no signal.

Booking mechanics
Southern AT · Recreation.gov
Nothing to do until 30 days out

Great Smoky Mountains moved its entire backcountry permit system to Recreation.gov on 30 April 2026. The old smokiespermits.nps.gov portal is retired. Any guide written before that date will send you to the wrong place.

The sequence

  1. Create a Recreation.gov account now. Free, takes two minutes, and it means you are not filling in forms on the morning your window opens. This is the only thing you can usefully do today.
  2. Set a calendar trigger for 30 days before your entry date. That is when the permit becomes bookable, not before. In November this is low season and you are unlikely to be racing anyone, but the trigger keeps it from slipping.
  3. Book the thru-hiker permit if you took the Hot Springs extension. $44.50, and it buys you a genuinely useful cushion: you may enter the park up to 15 days either side of your estimated entry date, a 30 day flex range that absorbs whatever the move does to your schedule.
  4. Or book the General Backcountry Permit if you kept it short. $8 per person per night, and you reserve a specific shelter for every single night inside the park. One night per shelter, no repeats, twelve AT shelters available.
  5. Buy a parking tag. Required for any vehicle parked in the park, separate from the permit. Since you are driving from Indiana this is not optional.
  6. Print the permit, not the confirmation. The checkout confirmation email is not your permit. Log back into Recreation.gov, open upcoming reservations, and print the actual permit, available up to 14 days before your trip. Rangers check in the field. Keep it on you and attach it at camp.
Nobody camps freely here, and this is the part people get wrong. Camping is permitted only at designated campsites and shelters, for every permit type. There is no dispersed camping anywhere in the park. What the thru-hiker permit buys is freedom from a fixed itinerary: you never declare which shelters you will use, you walk at whatever pace the weather allows, and if a shelter is full you may tent in the immediate area around it. A General permit locks you to named sites on named dates with no tenting fallback at all. That asymmetry is most of the case for the longer route.
General permit holders outrank thru-hikers, at any hour. A thru-hiker must surrender bunk space to anyone holding a shelter reservation regardless of what time that person arrives. If you walk the Hot Springs version on a thru-hiker permit, carry a shelter that can pitch on uneven ground beside a full shelter at 8pm in the cold, and treat the bunk as a bonus rather than a plan.
Call, do not guess. The Backcountry Office is 865-436-1297, 8am to 5pm Eastern, seven days a week. They will tell you current conditions, road status, and whether an itinerary adjustment is workable. Use them the week before you drive, and again if Newfound Gap Road is in weather. Book trailhead shuttles separately and directly with local operators.
Booking mechanics
Te Araroa · DOC and Immigration New Zealand
Almost nothing to book, which is the problem

There is no permit and no lottery. Richmond Ranges huts are standard, first-come, and cannot be reserved. That is exactly why nothing forces a decision date, and exactly why the conditions gate is the only thing standing between you and a bad November.

The sequence

  1. NZeTA and the visitor levy. Roughly NZ$17 through the mobile app or NZ$23 on the web, plus the NZ$100 International Visitor Levy in the same transaction. Allow 72 hours for processing. Do this before ticketing.
  2. Decide which pass. The Te Araroa Trail Pass is sold by Te Araroa Trust as part of walker registration and is required only if you are walking the full trail or the whole South Island. As a section walker it is optional. The DOC Backcountry Hut Pass is the alternative, bought through DOC's online booking system under "passes".
  3. Book the Nelson Lakes serviced huts individually. See the warning below. This is the step that catches people.
  4. Hire a personal locator beacon on arrival. Standard practice in New Zealand and widely available in Nelson and St Arnaud. Non-negotiable solo on this section.
  5. Print or download the pass PDF before you leave. It arrives by email after payment and links to your DOC account.
The trap on your exact route: from 1 October to 30 April the Backcountry Hut Pass does not cover serviced huts, and the Nelson Lakes huts on your line (Blue Lake, West Sabine, Upper Travers, John Tait, Lakehead) are all serviced. In your window they must be booked online and paid for individually. A hut pass alone does not get you a bed in Nelson Lakes in November.
Pass timing: the DOC Backcountry Hut Pass can only be bought up to one month before its first night of use, and the start date cannot be changed afterwards. If you buy the wrong dates you have 48 hours to cancel for a full refund. Carry a tent regardless of what you book. Tent equals guaranteed bed, hut equals upgrade.

Staying reachable

This applies whichever one you take, and it is the piece the original plan did not have at all.

The setup

  • Satellite communicator with two-way messaging, carried on your person and not in the pack lid. Share the tracking link with your parents and with Maddy before you leave.
  • One scheduled check-in per day at a fixed time, sent whether or not there is anything to say. The value is that its absence is information.
  • A written escalation note left with your parents: who to call, in what order, at what hour of missed contact, with the permit or booking references attached.
  • Kumano is the outlier here in a good way. Most nights you are in a village with signal and an innkeeper who knows you arrived.

Verify before you commit

  • Submit the Kumano Travel request for Miura-guchi and Koguchi first, early November dates, before anything else on this page gets decided.
  • Confirm your award routing out of IND, CVG or ORD before locking Japan lodging, not after.
  • Confirmed: both permit types open one month before the first night of your itinerary, through Recreation.gov. Still worth re-reading the page the week your window opens, since the system only changed hands in April 2026.
  • Check current NZeTA and levy amounts at immigration.govt.nz if New Zealand comes back into play.
  • Recheck all of it before booking. Prices, inventory, permit rules and transport schedules all move.
